Het perfectum
— de verleden tijd

The past tense with hebben or zijn + a participle: regular participles (soft ketchup), verbs without ge-, irregular verbs, and the homework from De opmaat (Oefening 9 p.98, Oefening 10 p.100).

Reference — het perfectum

Perfectum = two verbs
  1. The helping verb: hebben or zijn, in the present tense and in the right form for the subject (ik heb, hij heeft, ik ben, hij is…). It sits where the verb always sits.
  2. The main verb becomes a participle (voltooid deelwoord) and goes to the end. The participle is the same for all persons: ik heb gewerkt, wij hebben gewerkt.
Participle of regular verbs
  1. Find the base of the verb by removing -en (with the spelling rules from Les 2: maken → maak, kennen → ken, leven → leef).
  2. Add ge- in front (where that's necessary — see "no ge-" below).
  3. Add -t or -d at the end.
  4. To know if it's t or d, look at the letter before -en in the infinitive. Is it in soft ketchup(x)? → t. Not in it? → d.
SoFT KetCHuP (+X)  → t

The book also calls it 't ex-kofschip — same letters. Everything else (l, m, n, r, w, g, b, d, z, v, a vowel…) → d.

Let op: z and v → d

The rule looks at the infinitive, not the base. reizen has a z, proeven a v — not in soft ketchup → d, even though the base is written with s / f (reis, proef).

Base ends in t or d → add nothing

A Dutch word never ends in tt, dd, td or dt after ge-. If the base already ends in t or d, nothing is added.

No ge- : be-, ge-, ont-, ver-, her-, er-

If the verb begins with be- ge- ont- ver- her- er- and the stress is not on that first syllable (geBEUren, verTELlen), the participle gets no extra ge-. The rest of the rule stays the same.

Hebben or zijn?
  • Most verbs take hebben.
  • zijn for: zijn itself, and verbs of a change of place or state — gaan, komen, blijven, worden, beginnen, stoppen, gebeuren, veranderen, trouwen, slagen, zakken, stijgen, dalen… (book p.99). Learn them with their helping verb: komen → zijn gekomen, brengen → hebben gebracht.
  • Verbs of movement (lopen, fietsen, zwemmen, rijden): zijn when you go to a place (naar …), hebben when you just do the activity.
Word order (Les 3) with two verbs

The helping verb takes the place of the normal verb; the participle closes the sentence. The middle stays time – object – manner – place.

Irregular verbs (onregelmatige verba)

Many verbs are irregular: you can't build the participle yourself — look it up in the list (book p.125–127) and learn the common ones by heart. Most end in -en and change their vowel; a few end in -cht.
